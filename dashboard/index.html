The finding that decides this model

      
Amazon's AI capex earns its cost of capital, and nothing more, once you use Amazon's own
        disclosed contract life.

      
The model amortises AI-attributed backlog over an assumed 4.0-year life.
        At June 30 2026 Amazon disclosed a weighted-average remaining contract life of 6.4 years.
        The model keeps 4.0 for comparability with v01 — a shorter life recognises the same backlog faster, so it
        flatters the return. On Amazon's own number the latest run-rate spread all but
        disappears.

            Cross-company comparison
            
Capex is defined differently by each company

            
Five definitions sit in one denominator. Levels are not comparable across companies; only each company's
              own series over time is like-for-like.

            

              
MSFT
Management capex, including finance leases

              
GOOG
Cash purchases of property and equipment

              
AMZN
Gross productive-asset cash payments

              
ORCL
Cash capital expenditures (GAAP)

              
META
Company-defined capex incl. finance-lease principal

            

          


          

            Amazon
            
The 4.0-year duration is the largest single sensitivity

            
Amazon disclosed a 6.4-year weighted-average remaining contract life at June 30 2026. The model retains
              4.0 years. Switching to the disclosed figure collapses the latest run-rate
              spread from +447 bps to +7 bps,
              and the snapshot spread from +546 bps to
              +69 bps. The conclusion "Amazon earns above its cost of capital"
              does not survive the company's own disclosure.

          


          

            Alphabet
            
The backlog definition changed mid-series

            
Alphabet's revenue-backlog definition expanded in Q1 2026, and Q2 2026 Cloud backlog now includes TPU
              system-sale agreements. Q1 26 and Q2 26 are comparable with each other; the three 2025 quarters sit on the
              older definition. The step in the GOOG line across that boundary is partly definitional.

          


          

            Microsoft
            
The improved snapshot spread is partly an accounting artefact

            
The CY2026 capex outlook fell to about $175B from roughly $190B because longer datacenter useful lives
              shift future leases from finance leases (inside Microsoft's capex metric) to operating leases (outside it).
              Management said underlying investment expectations were unchanged. The denominator shrank; the spending did
              not. Read the run-rate trajectory alongside it.

          


          

            Oracle
            
The 85% AI share and 5-year duration are unverified

            
Oracle reports company-wide RPO. It does not disclose that 85% of it is AI-linked, nor that recognition is
              straight-line over five years. Both are analyst choices, and Oracle shows the widest modelled spread of the
              five. Cutting the AI share to 50% moves the latest run-rate spread from
              +2,505 bps to +1,151 bps; stretching
              duration to 7 years moves it to +1,566 bps. Oracle also disclosed $75B of prepaid or customer-supplied
              hardware that reduces its own capital requirement, which the denominator does not reflect.

          


          

            Cost of capital
            
WACC is a sector average, not a company number

            
Every WACC here is a Damodaran January 2026 U.S. industry average, chosen by the analyst from a published
              table. Alphabet and Meta share the same 10.66% Software (Internet) figure. These are not company-specific
              costs of capital, and the choice of sector is itself judgement.
              Damodaran table

          


          

            Meta
            
There is no backlog to amortise

            
Meta discloses no backlog, so the proxy annualises a single quarter of total company revenue and applies a
              20% AI attribution Meta does not report. That is a different economic object from the other four, and it is
              why Meta has no duration assumption — the page does not invent one.

          


          

            Annual denominators
            
The snapshot denominators are four different kinds of number

            
Alphabet ($200B) and Meta ($137.5B) are analyst midpoints of disclosed ranges. Oracle ($55.663B) is a
              completed FY2026 actual, used because its FY2027 guide is a non-comparable net-cash-outlay measure. Amazon
              ($200B) is a carried-forward plan with no Q2 replacement. Microsoft ($175B) is a reported outlook.
